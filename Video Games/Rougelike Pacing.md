# Rougelike Pacing: Hades vs Wizard of Legend
Mario vs Sonic. Fast rappers vs Slow Rappers. Speed.

Well this essay isn't about speed but it is about the speed... of pacing in rouge-like video games. 

Recently I been playing a lot of games together that feel pretty similar, at least on paper. 

For example, Wizard of Legend and Hades. Now both of these games are really, really fun, cream of the crop for their genre of these top-down combo-off beat-em-up hack-and-slash games (buzzword buzzword buzzword).

But I found Hades a lot more fun. 

And on paper, honestly, Wizard of Legend should be more fun for me. Hades pales in comparison to the move variety that is present in Wizard of Legend. I mean the number of builds that you can make in Wizard of legend is so much greater than the 8 weapons you get in Hades. And for me at least the boss fights in Wizard are a bit more fun as well. 

But I realized the reason I liked Hades a lot more was the pacing.

##### 1. New Items
The first difference is the rate of new items you get in the game. In Hades, you get a new item every time you clear the room. Sure, some of these items are ones that are to be used later in the game for upgrades between runs, but a lot of the time its upgrades from the gods or upgrades to your health, things that directly impact your current run. 

Wizard of Legend on the other hand doesn't do this. Usually the only time I can get new moves or upgrades that change how I play during a run is to go talk to the shopping dude, and usually the rate at which you get gold in that game, I barely end up having enough just to top my health off a bit, much less get a new move to spice up my run. 

So even though between runs Wizard of Legend got more variety with the move set, Hades wins in how your playstyle has those constant, subtle changes throughout the run. Even if the upgrades in Hades are samey every run, getting yet another dash AOE move will still guarantee to shake up the run itself, because I will be able to turn my dash into an offensive move when it wasn't earlier in the run. Hades also lets you switch up your playstyle by giving a place to replace your current charm after defeating each boss.

##### 2. The Map
The other thing that Hades does differently is the map.

In Wizard of Legend, the maps for each level are relatively big and often need to be explored pretty thoroughly. The game doesn't really tell you where the boss fight for the map is located so often times you will be walking in the "wrong" direction, and then you realize you did so you'll have to backtrack and find a new way through to the boss. Sometimes the game will give you a teleporter if you happened to find one of the shops (*where you can't buy anything because its all so damn expensive*). These will get you to the center of the map but sometimes you just hit a dead end and have to figure it out. Also sometimes you realized that a store you previously visited had an extra hallway, but because the portal gets overwritten with the most recent one that you discovered, you will have to back track all the way back up instead of just taking the teleporter just to find the boss there. This sprawling map also leads you to be checking the map pretty often and asking "wait did I do this way yet... or not?" and you do this like 3 to 5 times until you actually go down the path to the fun part of the game.  

Hades doesn't have this issue. There is no "wrong" direction. You get at most 2 choices of rooms to pick from and then just continue doing that until you hit a boss fight room. The game never has that issue of backtracking and checking your map, where you are going through empty rooms doing nothing but walking and going "did I go here yet?". You are always moving in the right direction and always getting enemies to fight and getting them quickly.


##### 3. Another comparison for funsies
My feelings about why I prefer the top down shooter Nuclear Throne to Enter the Gungeon are basically the same. Nuclear Throne always showers you weapons throughout the level and with mutations at the end of each level that change up the way you play, while Gungeon is more conservative with its new weapon drops. And Nuclear Throne also doesn't make you as the player backtrack or think about where the next encounter is, since the maps in Nuclear Throne are so small and after clearing each you just get teleported to the next one, while in Gungeon you're often backtracking because you went the way opposite of where the damn boss actually is. 

Interestingly enough, I do think Gungeon has the better boss fights than Nuclear Throne as well, so maybe something about these slow paced games makes boss fights feel a bit more special, probably because getting to them is a bit harder on a given run or maybe just by coincidence they do genuinely have the better design I don't know.

So to summarize all this, you make your rouge-like pace faster by:
1. Removing the need for backtracking from the game and always keeping that steady state of new encounters flowing throughout the game, which you do by basically making it so that the player cannot move in a "negative" direction so to speak
2. Giving the player a steady flow of new items
3. Making sure that the new items that you give the player at least slightly change how they approach encounters and their strategy in each room
	- What is important about this point is that the items can actually be quite samey throughout runs. The upgrades in Hades and weapons in Nuclear throne oftentimes play very similarly, I mean the difference between Aphrodite's Dash vs Dioneses' Dash (Hades) or the AR vs the Pop Gun (Nuclear Throne) are not that different. What matters is that I have to change my shotgun for an AR in Nuclear Throne and that I'm turning my dash into an offensive tool MID RUN. That subtle change of playstyle MID RUN is what matters when it comes to this point.

Also, it's important to note these slow paced games can kind of get around their slow pace through the use of teleporters, like Gungeon and Wizard of Legend, but these games still force you to at least backtrack the portion of the map that requires you to get to the teleporter, which still has that same issue.

So we kinda get how a rouge like can make its pace faster. But why, why do at least I, prefer this faster pace. When a game is super fast all the time, I honestly don't have a chance to get bored. I'm always entertained and I don't even have a moment to think about all the other games that I have to play or even the fact that I could be playing other games. Also, faster paced games make losing less annoying, since getting to the part where I died will be much faster. Also, removing this friction between runs is incredibly important for any rouge-like if they want to be one of those super addicting ones where players want to do like 5 runs in one sitting. The other thing is that when I play an action packed game, I want that feeling of action you know? If I wanted a break from the game I would turn it off and go play something more chill, but I like my doses of action to be back - to - back for about 30 to 90 minutes, instead of 2 to 3 hours of drip fed action where I have to be checking my map half the time to see where I'm supposed to go next.
