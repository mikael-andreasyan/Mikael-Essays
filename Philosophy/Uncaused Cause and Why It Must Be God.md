## Aquianas' First Cause and Why It Must Be God
One of the most famous arguments presented by Thomas Aquinas is the uncaused cause (or first cause) argument. I'm not going to go too much into detail about it since there are so many resources on it.

However, one thing Aquinas does not do is actually argue how the uncaused cause *must* be God. However if we actually reason through this, we can come to the conclusion that *if* Aquinas is correct that infinite causal chains are impossible and an uncaused cause must exist to explain the universe, then said cause *must* be an agent with free will (though this doesn't establish the Christian God nor a God with the omni properties).

**Core idea:** Let's suppose we prove that infinite causal chains are impossible and that an uncaused cause must exist. Anything that is not an agent can only change, or cause change in something else, if a prior cause acts on it first. So here is the argument:

1. In order for a non-agent to cause something, it must first be changed or moved by a prior cause.
2. The chain of causes cannot go back infinitely (by supposition), so there must be a first cause.
3. A first cause cannot have a prior cause.
4. The universe and matter are constantly changing, and they are non-agents.
5. Therefore, the material universe cannot be the first cause, because something must have caused it to begin changing.
6. So the first cause must be able to begin change on its own, without being moved by anything prior.
7. The only kind of thing that can begin change on its own is an agent, a being that acts by its own will.
---
**Conclusion: Therefore, the first cause of the universe is an agent.**

Interestingly enough, if this argument holds, it also establishes that free will necessarily exists as well. So realistically, for the athiest or materialist, they *have* to accept that an infinite causal chain must exist, because otherwise they accept the existence of some God-like figure (though not necessarily the Christian or the omni-God)
